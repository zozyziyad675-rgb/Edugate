20 MCQs from Lecture 3: Probability Distributions

---

Q1. According to the probability distributions tree, which of the following is a discrete distribution?
A) Normal
B) Student's t
C) Poisson
D) Chi-Square
Answer: C) Poisson

---

Q2. The Binomial distribution models the number of successes in:
A) An infinite number of trials
B) A fixed number of independent Bernoulli trials
C) A Poisson process
D) A continuous interval
Answer: B) A fixed number of independent Bernoulli trials

---

Q3. For a binomial random variable with parameters n and p, the mean is:
A) np(1-p)
B) np
C) p/n
D) n/p
Answer: B) np

---

Q4. For a binomial random variable, the variance is:
A) np
B) np(1-p)
C) n(1-p)
D) p(1-p)
Answer: B) np(1-p)

---

Q5. The binomial PMF is given by:
A) \lambda^x e^{-\lambda}/x!
B) \binom{n}{x} p^x (1-p)^{n-x}
C) \frac{1}{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-(x-\mu)^2/2\sigma^2}
D) p^x (1-p)^x
Answer: B) \binom{n}{x} p^x (1-p)^{n-x}

---

Q6. The Poisson distribution expresses the probability of a given number of events occurring within:
A) A fixed number of trials
B) A fixed interval of time or space
C) A continuous range
D) A standard normal curve
Answer: B) A fixed interval of time or space

---

Q7. A unique property of the Poisson distribution is that:
A) Mean > Variance
B) Mean < Variance
C) Mean = Variance = \lambda
D) Mean and variance are unrelated
Answer: C) Mean = Variance = \lambda

---

Q8. The Poisson PMF is:
A) \binom{n}{x} p^x (1-p)^{n-x}
B) \lambda^x e^{-\lambda}/x!
C) \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-z^2/2}
D) np(1-p)
Answer: B) \lambda^x e^{-\lambda}/x!

---

Q9. Which of the following is a continuous distribution?
A) Binomial
B) Poisson
C) Normal
D) None of the above
Answer: C) Normal

---

Q10. The normal density function involves the parameters:
A) n and p
B) \lambda
C) \mu and \sigma
D) k and N
Answer: C) \mu and \sigma

---

Q11. The standard normal distribution has:
A) Mean = 1, SD = 0
B) Mean = 0, SD = 1
C) Mean = 0, SD = 0
D) Mean = 1, SD = 1
Answer: B) Mean = 0, SD = 1

---

Q12. The z-score transformation is given by:
A) Z = (Y - \mu)/\sigma
B) Z = (\mu - Y)/\sigma
C) Z = \sigma/(Y - \mu)
D) Z = Y/\sigma
Answer: A) Z = (Y - \mu)/\sigma

---

Q13. For a normal distribution with \mu = 125 and \sigma = 13.7, the z-score for X = 110 is approximately:
A) 1.09
B) −1.09
C) 5.29
D) −5.29
Answer: B) −1.09

---

Q14. For the blood pressure example, P(X > 197.5) is approximately:
A) 0.1379
B) 0.7242
C) 0.0062
D) 0.0000 (beyond table range, z ≈ 5.29)
Answer: D) 0.0000

---

Q15. For the blood pressure example, P(X < 110) = P(Z < -1.09) equals:
A) 0.1379
B) 0.8621
C) 0.7242
D) 0.0062
Answer: A) 0.1379 (by symmetry, P(Z > 1.09))

---

Q16. For the blood pressure example, P(110 < X < 140) = P(-1.09 < Z < 1.09) equals:
A) 0.1379
B) 0.7242
C) 0.8621
D) 0.0062
Answer: B) 0.7242

---

Q17. The Central Limit Theorem states that as n \to \infty, the distribution of Z = (\bar{X} - \mu)/(\sigma/\sqrt{n}) approaches:
A) A binomial distribution
B) A Poisson distribution
C) The standard normal distribution
D) A chi-square distribution
Answer: C) The standard normal distribution

---

Q18. A numerical quantity that describes a characteristic of a population is called a:
A) Statistic
B) Parameter
C) Sample
D) Variable
Answer: B) Parameter

---

Q19. The standard deviation of the sample mean \bar{X} (standard error) is:
A) \sigma
B) \sigma^2
C) \sigma/\sqrt{n}
D) \sigma/n
Answer: C) \sigma/\sqrt{n}

---

Q20. In the resistor example (\mu = 100, \sigma = 10, n = 25), P(\bar{X} < 95) equals:
A) 0.0062
B) 0.1379
C) 0.7242
D) 0.0228
Answer: A) 0.0062 (z = −2.5)
