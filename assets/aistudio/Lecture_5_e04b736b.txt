Lecture 5: Inverse Laplace Transform (Part 2) — Complete Explanation

🎯 What This Lecture Is About

This lecture continues the Inverse Laplace Transform. It covers four major tools:

1. Completing the square (for quadratic denominators)
2. Theorem 4: Derivative of F(s) (for logs and arctan)
3. Theorem 3: Second Shifting Property (for e^{-as}F(s))
4. Partial Fractions (non-repeated and repeated linear factors)

Let's go through every page carefully.

---

📄 Page 2: Completing the Square

Example 1: \displaystyle F(s)=\frac{1}{s^2+4}

Key idea: Match the denominator to a standard table form. The standard form is:

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\}=\sin \omega t

Here \omega^2=4, so \omega=2. We need a "2" on top, so we multiply and divide by 2:

F(s)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{2}{s^2+4}

\boxed{L^{-1}\{F(s)\}=\frac{1}{2}\sin 2t}

⚠️ Important: Never forget the factor \frac{1}{\omega}. The table needs \omega in the numerator.

Example 2: \displaystyle F(s)=\frac{1}{s^2+4s+3}

Step 1 — Complete the square on the denominator:

s^2+4s+3 = s^2+4s+4-4+3 = (s+2)^2-1

(We added and subtracted 4 because \left(\frac{4}{2}\right)^2=4.)

Step 2 — Rewrite:

F(s)=\frac{1}{(s+2)^2-1}

Step 3 — Use the First Shifting Property:

L^{-1}\{F(s-a)\}=e^{at}f(t) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L^{-1}\{G(s+2)\}=e^{-2t}L^{-1}\{G(s)\}

Here G(s)=\frac{1}{s^2-1}, and from the table:

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^2-1}\right\}=\sinh t

Therefore:

\boxed{L^{-1}\{F(s)\}=e^{-2t}\sinh t}

💡 Why \sinh and not \sin? Because the denominator is s^2-1 (a minus sign), which matches \sinh (or \sin with imaginary argument). A plus sign (s^2+1) gives \sin.

Summary of standard forms used:

F(s) f(t)
\dfrac{1}{s^2+\omega^2} \dfrac{1}{\omega}\sin\omega t
\dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} \cos\omega t
\dfrac{1}{s^2-\omega^2} \dfrac{1}{\omega}\sinh\omega t
\dfrac{s}{s^2-\omega^2} \cosh\omega t
\dfrac{1}{s^n} \dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}

---

📄 Page 3: Theorem 4 — Derivative of F(s)

The Theorem

\boxed{L^{-1}\left\{\frac{dF(s)}{ds}\right\}=-t\,f(t)}

where f(t)=L^{-1}\{F(s)\}.

🔑 How to use it: If F(s) is a logarithm or arctan, you cannot find it directly in the table. So:

1. Compute F'(s).
2. Find L^{-1}\{F'(s)\} (this is usually easy!).
3. Divide by -t to get f(t).

Example 5(i): F(s)=\ln(s+3)

Step 1 — Differentiate:

F'(s)=\frac{1}{s+3}

Step 2 — Inverse transform:

L^{-1}\{F'(s)\}=L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s+3}\right\}=e^{-3t}

Step 3 — Apply Theorem 4:

-t\,f(t)=e^{-3t}

\boxed{f(t)=-\frac{e^{-3t}}{t}}

⚠️ Common mistake: Forgetting the minus sign from -tf(t).

---

📄 Page 4: More Theorem 4 Examples

Example 5(ii): \displaystyle F(s)=\ln\left(1+\frac{1}{s^2}\right)

Step 1 — Simplify using log laws:

F(s)=\ln\left(\frac{s^2+1}{s^2}\right)=\ln(s^2+1)-2\ln s

Step 2 — Differentiate:

F'(s)=\frac{2s}{s^2+1}-\frac{2}{s}

Step 3 — Inverse transform term by term:

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{2s}{s^2+1}\right\}=2\cos t

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{2}{s}\right\}=2

So:

L^{-1}\{F'(s)\}=2\cos t-2

Step 4 — Apply Theorem 4:

-t\,f(t)=2\cos t-2

\boxed{f(t)=\frac{2-2\cos t}{t}}

💡 Notice how the minus sign flips the numerator. Always divide by -t.

Example 5(iii): F(s)=\tan^{-1}s

Step 1 — Differentiate (standard derivative):

F'(s)=\frac{1}{s^2+1}

Step 2 — Inverse transform:

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^2+1}\right\}=\sin t

Step 3 — Apply Theorem 4:

-t\,f(t)=\sin t

\boxed{f(t)=-\frac{\sin t}{t}}

🎯 Pattern to remember: For these problems, the answer is always \dfrac{\text{(something)}}{t}.

---

📄 Page 5: Theorem 3 — Second Shifting Property

The Theorem

\boxed{L^{-1}\{e^{-as}F(s)\}=u(t-a)\,f(t-a)}

where f(t)=L^{-1}\{F(s)\} and u(t-a) is the unit step function (Heaviside function):

u(t-a)=\begin{cases}0, & t<a\\ 1, & t\ge a\end{cases}

🔑 Recipe:

1. Remove the exponential e^{-as}.
2. Find the inverse of what remains: f(t).
3. Replace every t by (t-a).
4. Multiply by u(t-a).

Example 4(i): \displaystyle \frac{e^{-5s}}{(s+3)^3}

Step 1 — Ignore the exponential, work with F(s)=\dfrac{1}{(s+3)^3}.

Step 2 — First Shifting Property:
L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(s+3)^3}\right\}=e^{-3t}L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^3}\right\}

Since L^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^3}\right\}=\dfrac{t^2}{2!}=\dfrac{t^2}{2}:

f(t)=\frac{t^2e^{-3t}}{2}

Step 3 — Apply Second Shifting with a=5: replace t\to(t-5), multiply by u(t-5):

\boxed{L^{-1}\left\{\frac{e^{-5s}}{(s+3)^3}\right\}=u(t-5)\cdot\frac{(t-5)^2e^{-3(t-5)}}{2}}

⚠️ Critical: The e^{-3t} also gets shifted! It becomes e^{-3(t-5)}, not e^{-3t}.

---

📄 Page 6: More Second Shifting Examples

Example 4(ii): \displaystyle \frac{e^{-4s}}{s^2}

Step 1 — Base function:

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^2}\right\}=t

So f(t)=t.

Step 2 — Shift with a=4:

\boxed{L^{-1}\left\{\frac{e^{-4s}}{s^2}\right\}=(t-4)\,u(t-4)}

Example 4(iii): \displaystyle \frac{e^{-3s}}{s^2+9}

Step 1 — Base function:

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^2+9}\right\}=\frac{1}{3}\sin 3t

Step 2 — Shift with a=3: replace t\to(t-3), multiply by u(t-3):

\boxed{L^{-1}\left\{\frac{e^{-3s}}{s^2+9}\right\}=\frac{1}{3}u(t-3)\sin 3(t-3)}

💡 Remember: The \frac{1}{3} factor stays in front; only t is shifted.

📄 Page 7: Partial Fractions — Overview

We now handle rational functions \dfrac{P(s)}{Q(s)} where:

· P(s) and Q(s) are polynomials
· \deg P < \deg Q (proper fraction)
· Coefficients are real numbers

The form of the expansion depends on the factors of Q(s). Two cases:

1. Non-repeated linear factors
2. Repeated linear factors

📌 If \deg P \ge \deg Q, you must first do long division.

---

📄 Page 8: Case 1 — Non-Repeated Linear Factors

If:

Q(s)=(s-s_1)(s-s_2)\cdots(s-s_n)

with all s_i distinct real numbers, then:

\boxed{\frac{P(s)}{Q(s)}=\frac{A_1}{s-s_1}+\frac{A_2}{s-s_2}+\cdots+\frac{A_n}{s-s_n}}

Two methods to find the constants:

Method A — Substitution (cover-up): Multiply both sides by the full denominator, then substitute s=s_i to isolate each constant.

Method B — Comparing coefficients: Expand and equate coefficients of like powers of s.

💡 Inverse transforms you'll need:

> L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s-a}\right\}=e^{at}
> 

---

📄 Page 9: Example 1 — Three Distinct Linear Factors

Problem

F(s)=\frac{s+3}{s(s-1)(s+2)}

Step 1 — Set up the expansion

\frac{s+3}{s(s-1)(s+2)}=\frac{A}{s}+\frac{B}{s-1}+\frac{C}{s+2}

Step 2 — Multiply by s(s-1)(s+2)

s+3=A(s-1)(s+2)+Bs(s+2)+Cs(s-1)

Step 3 — Substitute smart values of s

Let s=0:

3=A(-1)(2)+0+0=-2A \implies A=-\frac{3}{2}

Let s=1:

4=0+B(1)(3)+0=3B \implies B=\frac{4}{3}

Let s=-2:

1=0+0+C(-2)(-3)=6C \implies C=\frac{1}{6}

Result

F(s)=\frac{-3/2}{s}+\frac{4/3}{s-1}+\frac{1/6}{s+2}

---

📄 Page 10: Inverse Transform of Example 1

L^{-1}\{F(s)\}=L^{-1}\left\{\frac{-3/2}{s}\right\}+L^{-1}\left\{\frac{4/3}{s-1}\right\}+L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1/6}{s+2}\right\}

Using L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s}\right\}=1, L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s-1}\right\}=e^{t}, L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s+2}\right\}=e^{-2t}:

\boxed{L^{-1}\{F(s)\}=-\frac{3}{2}+\frac{4}{3}e^{t}+\frac{1}{6}e^{-2t}}

⚠️ Note: \frac{1}{s+2}=\frac{1}{s-(-2)}, so the exponent is -2t, not +2t.

Example 2: \displaystyle F(s)=\frac{2s-1}{s^3-s}

Step 1 — Factor the denominator:

s^3-s=s(s^2-1)=s(s-1)(s+1)

Step 2 — Set up:

\frac{2s-1}{s(s-1)(s+1)}=\frac{A}{s}+\frac{B}{s-1}+\frac{C}{s+1}

---

📄 Page 11: Solving Example 2

Multiply by s(s-1)(s+1):

2s-1=A(s-1)(s+1)+Bs(s+1)+Cs(s-1)

Let s=0:

-1=A(-1)(1) \implies A=1

Let s=1:

1=B(1)(2) \implies B=\frac{1}{2}

Let s=-1:

-3=C(-1)(-2)=2C \implies C=-\frac{3}{2}

So:

F(s)=\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1/2}{s-1}-\frac{3/2}{s+1}

Therefore (combining with Page 12):

\boxed{f(t)=1+\frac{1}{2}e^{t}-\frac{3}{2}e^{-t}}

---

📄 Page 12: Case 2 — Repeated Linear Factors

If (s-s_k) is a factor of Q(s) with power n, then that part of the expansion is:

\boxed{\frac{A_1}{s-s_k}+\frac{A_2}{(s-s_k)^2}+\cdots+\frac{A_n}{(s-s_k)^n}}

🔑 Rule: A repeated factor of power n produces n terms, with denominators of increasing power 1,2,\dots,n.

💡 Inverse transforms needed:

> L^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{(s-a)^n}\right\}=\frac{t^{n-1}e^{at}}{(n-1)!}
> 

Example 3: \displaystyle F(s)=\frac{s^2+9s+2}{(s-1)^2(s+3)}

The factor (s-1) is repeated twice, so:
\frac{s^2+9s+2}{(s-1)^2(s+3)}=\frac{A}{s-1}+\frac{B}{(s-1)^2}+\frac{C}{s+3}

---

📄 Page 13: Solving Example 3

Multiply by (s-1)^2(s+3):

s^2+9s+2=A(s-1)(s+3)+B(s+3)+C(s-1)^2

Let s=1 (kills A and C):

1+9+2=B(4) \implies 12=4B \implies B=3

Let s=-3 (kills A and B):

9-27+2=C(-4)^2 \implies -16=16C \implies C=-1

Compare coefficients of s^2:

· LHS: 1
· RHS: A(s^2+\dots)+C(s^2+\dots) → coefficient is A+C

So:

1=A+C \implies 1=A-1 \implies A=2

Final expansion

F(s)=\frac{2}{s-1}+\frac{3}{(s-1)^2}-\frac{1}{s+3}

Inverse transform

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{2}{s-1}\right\}=2e^{t}

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{3}{(s-1)^2}\right\}=3te^{t}

L^{-1}\left\{\frac{-1}{s+3}\right\}=-e^{-3t}

\boxed{f(t)=2e^{t}+3te^{t}-e^{-3t}}

💡 Check: \frac{1}{(s-1)^2} gives te^{t} (since n=2: \frac{t^{2-1}e^{t}}{(2-1)!}=te^{t}). The repeated factor always produces a t multiplier.

⚠️ Top 5 Mistakes to Avoid

1. Forgetting \frac{1}{\omega} when inverting \frac{1}{s^2+\omega^2}.
2. Wrong sign in Theorem 4: f(t)=\dfrac{L^{-1}\{F'(s)\}}{-t}.
3. Not shifting the exponential in Second Shifting: e^{-3t}\to e^{-3(t-5)}, not e^{-3t}.
4. Forgetting u(t-a) entirely.
5. Missing terms in repeated factors: power n needs n terms.
