20 MCQs from Lecture 2: Measures of Central Tendency and Dispersion

---

Q1. The arithmetic mean is defined as:
A) The middle value of an ordered dataset
B) The sum of all data values divided by the total number of observations
C) The most frequently occurring value
D) The difference between the largest and smallest values
Answer: B) The sum of all data values divided by the total number of observations

---

Q2. For a sample of five heights (65.51, 72.30, 68.31, 67.05, 70.68 inches), the sample mean is:
A) 67.05 in.
B) 68.31 in.
C) 68.77 in.
D) 70.68 in.
Answer: C) 68.77 in.

---

Q3. When n is odd, the median position is determined by:
A) n/2
B) (n+1)/2
C) (n-1)/2
D) 3(n+1)/4
Answer: B) (n+1)/2

---

Q4. For the five heights (65.51, 72.30, 68.31, 67.05, 70.68), the sample median is:
A) 65.51
B) 67.05
C) 68.31
D) 70.68
Answer: C) 68.31

---

Q5. Quartiles divide an ordered dataset into:
A) Two equal parts
B) Four equal parts
C) Ten equal parts
D) One hundred equal parts
Answer: B) Four equal parts

---

Q6. The second quartile (Q_2) is identical to:
A) The mode
B) The median
C) The range
D) The standard deviation
Answer: B) The median

---

Q7. The position of the k-th percentile (P_k) is approximately:
A) k(n+1)/4
B) k(n+1)/10
C) k(n+1)/100
D) k(n+1)/2
Answer: C) k(n+1)/100

---

Q8. The fifth decile (D_5) is identical to:
A) Q_1
B) Q_3
C) The median
D) P_1
Answer: C) The median

---

Q9. For the dataset 26, 25, 25, 34, the mode is:
A) 25
B) 26
C) 34
D) No mode
Answer: A) 25

---

Q10. For the dataset 3, 7, 12, 6, 19, the mode is:
A) 3
B) 7
C) 19
D) No mode
Answer: D) No mode (all values occur once)

---

Q11. For the dataset 3, 3, 12, 6, 8, 8, the dataset is:
A) Unimodal
B) Bimodal (3 and 8)
C) Multimodal
D) No mode
Answer: B) Bimodal (3 and 8)

---

Q12. In a symmetric distribution:
A) Mean > Median > Mode
B) Mean < Median < Mode
C) Mode = Mean = Median
D) Mode > Mean > Median
Answer: C) Mode = Mean = Median

---

Q13. In a negatively skewed (skewed left) distribution:
A) Mean > Median > Mode
B) Mean < Median < Mode
C) Mode = Mean = Median
D) Mode < Median < Mean
Answer: B) Mean < Median < Mode

---

Q14. In a positively skewed (skewed right) distribution:
A) Mean > Median > Mode
B) Mean < Median < Mode
C) Mode = Mean = Median
D) Mode > Median > Mean
Answer: A) Mean > Median > Mode

---

Q15. The range is defined as:
A) The average squared deviation from the mean
B) The positive square root of the variance
C) The difference between the maximum and minimum values
D) The standard deviation as a percentage of the mean
Answer: C) The difference between the maximum and minimum values

---

Q16. The sample variance s^2 for raw data uses which denominator?
A) n
B) n-1
C) n+1
D) N
Answer: B) n-1 (degrees of freedom)

---

Q17. The standard deviation is defined as:
A) The square of the variance
B) The positive square root of the variance
C) The difference between max and min
D) The average of all deviations
Answer: B) The positive square root of the variance

---

Q18. The coefficient of variation (CV) is calculated as:
A) s \times \bar{x} \times 100\%
B) \bar{x} / s \times 100\%
C) s / \bar{x} \times 100\%
D) s^2 / \bar{x} \times 100\%
Answer: C) s / \bar{x} \times 100\%

---

Q19. For grouped data, the mean is calculated using:
A) \sum x_i / n
B) \sum x_i f_i / \sum f_i
C) \sum f_i / \sum x_i
D) \sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 / N
Answer: B) \sum x_i f_i / \sum f_i, where x_i is the class mark

---

Q20. In the grouped data example (classes 0–8, 8–16, …, 40–48 with frequencies 8, 10, 16, 24, 15, 7; N = 80), the mean is:
A) 22.5
B) 24.9
C) 26.0
D) 27.8
Answer: B) 24.9 (1992/80)
