Here are 20 multiple-choice questions based on Lecture 05 (Eliminations and the Inverse of a Matrix).

Q1. To find the inverse of an n \times n matrix A using Gauss-Jordan elimination, we form the matrix:
A) [A \mid 0]
B) [I_n \mid A]
C) [A \mid I_n]
D) [A \mid A^T]
Answer: C) [A \mid I_n]

Q2. If Gauss-Jordan elimination on [A \mid I_n] produces [I_n \mid B], then:
A) B = A^T
B) B = A^{-1}
C) B = I_n
D) A is singular
Answer: B) B = A^{-1}

Q3. If during Gauss-Jordan elimination on [A \mid I_n] a row of zeros appears on the left side (A portion), we conclude that:
A) A is invertible
B) A is singular (has no inverse)
C) B = I_n
D) the system has infinitely many solutions
Answer: B) A is singular (has no inverse)

Q4. Which of the following is NOT one of the equivalent statements for an n \times n matrix A?
A) A is nonsingular
B) A is row equivalent to I_n
C) Ax = b has a unique solution for every b
D) Ax = 0 has infinitely many solutions
Answer: D) Ax = 0 has infinitely many solutions (it should have only the trivial solution)

Q5. If A is an n \times n nonsingular matrix, the homogeneous system Ax = 0 has:
A) infinitely many solutions
B) no solution
C) only the trivial solution x = 0
D) exactly two solutions
Answer: C) only the trivial solution x = 0

Q6. If A is nonsingular, the unique solution of Ax = b is:
A) x = bA^{-1}
B) x = A^{-1}b
C) x = Ab
D) x = A^T b
Answer: B) x = A^{-1}b

Q7. To find the inverse of A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}, we solve AX = I, which can be split into:
A) one system with two right-hand sides
B) two systems sharing the same coefficient matrix A
C) a homogeneous system only
D) an inconsistent system
Answer: B) two systems sharing the same coefficient matrix A

Q8. The "doubly augmented" matrix used to find A^{-1} has size:
A) n \times n
B) n \times 2n
C) 2n \times n
D) 2n \times 2n
Answer: B) n \times 2n

Q9. If A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix}, its inverse is:
A) \begin{bmatrix} 5 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
B) \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}
C) \begin{bmatrix} -5 & -3 \\ -2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}
D) \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
Answer: B) \begin{bmatrix} -5 & 3 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix} (since |A| = -1, A^{-1} = \frac{1}{-1}\begin{bmatrix} 5 & -3 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix})

Q10. The process of solving two systems with the same coefficient matrix simultaneously is done by:
A) adjoining the identity matrix to the coefficient matrix
B) adjoining the two right-hand sides together
C) transposing the matrix
D) using determinants only
Answer: A) adjoining the identity matrix to the coefficient matrix

Q11. In Example 3, the matrix is shown to be singular because:
A) a row of zeros appears on the left side of [A \mid I]
B) the identity matrix appears on the left
C) the determinant is nonzero
D) it has a unique inverse
Answer: A) a row of zeros appears on the left side of [A \mid I]

Q12. In Example 4, the coefficient matrix for both systems is:
A) \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 & -5 \end{bmatrix}
B) \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
C) \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
D) \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 & -5 \end{bmatrix}
Answer: A) \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 3 & -5 \end{bmatrix}

Q13. For the system in Example 4(a) with right-hand side b = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, the solution is:
A) x_1 = 1, x_2 = 2, x_3 = 0
B) x_1 = 0, x_2 = 2, x_3 = 1
C) x_1 = 2, x_2 = 1, x_3 = 0
D) x_1 = 3, x_2 = 1, x_3 = 1
Answer: B) x_1 = 0, x_2 = 2, x_3 = 1

Q14. For the homogeneous system in Example 4(b), the solution is:
A) infinitely many solutions
B) no solution
C) the trivial solution x = 0
D) x = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
Answer: C) the trivial solution x = 0

Q15. In Example 2 (3×3 inverse), after row reduction, the inverse A^{-1} was found to be:
A) \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 2 & -1 \\ 1 & -3/2 & 1/2 \\ 1/2 & -1/2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} multiplied by \frac{1}{2}
B) \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}
C) the zero matrix
D) not possible to find
Answer: A) \begin{bmatrix} -2 & 2 & -1 \\ 1 & -3/2 & 1/2 \\ 1/2 & -1/2 & 0 \end{bmatrix} multiplied by \frac{1}{2} (equivalently \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} -1 & 4 & -1 \\ 2 & -3 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix})

Q16. The check for the inverse is performed by verifying:
A) A + A^{-1} = I
B) AA^{-1} = I_n
C) A - A^{-1} = 0
D) A^T = A^{-1}
Answer: B) AA^{-1} = I_n

Q17. A square matrix that can be transformed to the identity matrix by row operations is:
A) singular
B) nonsingular (invertible)
C) skew-symmetric
D) homogeneous
Answer: B) nonsingular (invertible)

Q18. If A is invertible and Ax = b, then the solution set is:
A) empty
B) a single unique vector
C) infinitely many vectors
D) the zero vector only
Answer: B) a single unique vector

Q19. When solving AX = I for a 2 \times 2 matrix A, the matrix X has size:
A) 1 \times 2
B) 2 \times 1
C) 2 \times 2
D) 1 \times 1
Answer: C) 2 \times 2

Q20. If a matrix A is singular, which of the following is true?
A) A is row equivalent to I_n
B) A has an inverse
C) A cannot be row reduced to I_n
D) Ax = 0 has only the trivial solution
Answer: C) A cannot be row reduced to I_n
